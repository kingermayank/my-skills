# Campaign, mockups, motion, and case study

## Prove the system in a real application

Choose touchpoints from audience behaviour: who encounters the brand first, where, and what should they feel or do within a few seconds? Use applications that test the system rather than a standard collection of attractive merchandise.

For a campaign, develop one clear message before its variations. Compare short headlines against audience tension, the actual offer, voice, differentiation, and comprehension. Show what changes between executions and what holds the campaign together.

The course's “6-sheet” assignment means **one outdoor poster format**, such as a bus-shelter panel; it does not mean six posters. Confirm the actual placement and supplier dimensions when producing print artwork. Six consistent image variations are a separate course exercise, not a poster-count requirement.

Build flat artwork first with the real logo, type, colour, message, and image treatment. Test hierarchy at thumbnail size and at intended viewing distance; for outdoor work, ask whether the core idea reads in roughly three seconds. Inspect mandatory copy and production specifications for the actual job. A photorealistic mockup can conceal a weak flat design.

## Make mockups believable and accurate

Generate or choose an unbranded scene with relevant materials, camera angle, light, and room for artwork. Reuse a shared style reference or scene specification across the set. Composite the approved artwork as a separate layer or smart object where exact typography, geometry, or colour matters.

Match perspective, curvature, scale, masks/occlusion, surface texture, reflections, and light. A multiply blend can help on suitable light paper surfaces, but inspect the result rather than applying it universally. Keep a clean source artwork file and an editable composite. Distinguish concept visualisation from supplier-ready production or an actual photographed installation.

## Give motion one clear job

Choose a purposeful brand behaviour: subtle environmental movement, a restrained reveal, kinetic language, a product action, or a short world-building clip. Decide the format and duration before running a paid model. Start with one action and one camera idea.

Use five briefing dimensions:

1. **Motion style:** rhythm, tempo, easing, duration, loop versus one-shot.
2. **Subject:** what moves and its single primary action.
3. **Composition:** framing, lens/depth, camera movement and amount, safe space.
4. **Environment:** setting, time, surface, light, atmosphere.
5. **Art direction:** colour/contrast/texture, stable end frame or loop behaviour.

Define what must remain still and exact. For image-to-video, protect approved logos, type, packaging, and layout. A short motion prompt can be more controllable than a long narrative. Use start/end frames when supported and appropriate; they are constraints to test, not a guarantee of accurate interpolation.

A portable original example: “A slow three-second light sweep across the unbranded metal surface, static camera, restrained contrast. Preserve the product silhouette and scene geometry. The separate logo overlay remains sharp and stationary; finish on a stable frame.”

When exact marks or letters warp, animate or composite those elements deterministically rather than accepting broken identity assets. Keep trials small; change one variable, compare with the same source frame, and stop when the requested motion is usable.

Inspect opening/middle/closing frames and actual playback, not just a poster frame. Check temporal drift, new objects, warped marks, unwanted camera movement, and loop seams. Export to the actual channel's dimensions, duration, codec, and size requirements. Provide a still fallback where useful.

## Present the reasoning and the work together

A full case study can connect problem/context → concise strategy → territory → identity → campaign → selected rollouts → motion → outcomes/learnings → credits. Include process evidence where it explains a meaningful decision, not as a prompt dump.

The course's final exercise combines strategy, territory boards, a primary logo, a locked poster, 2–3 relevant rollout items, and one motion element. Treat these as an adaptable completeness model; they do not expand a narrower user assignment. The course upload limit and classroom deadlines are not general delivery requirements.

Use consistent type scales, spacing, colour ratios, image treatment, and motion rules across the presentation. Inspect mobile legibility when relevant. Distinguish demonstrated deliverables and actual results from intended benefits or untested outcomes. Finish with editable sources, exports, a continuation record, and the provenance needed for the project.
